- Rulings / conservative interpretations:
  - Per fill per leg (brief ruling 1): rate × |q·P| at the fill price, the entry on 1 and |β|, the exit on |q1·P1x| and |q2·P2x|; charged on its own fill date in the daily MTM (`cost_entry`, `cost_exit`). The almgren per-fill rate is the existing `leg_cost_log` of the leg, computed once per window from the training rows (σ, ADV at the training end) and used for both fills, on the leg's own notional (Ruling R38, fix round 1): leg 1 at `--notional`, leg 2 at |β_e|·notional with β_e the trade's entry β (per trade; on the Kalman path β of the entry decision row); the entry participation sets the rate of both fills (the exit notional's effect on participation is second order).
  - Borrow: on each held row (both legs print, (entry fill, exit fill], as T4) for each short leg at rate/252 × |q·P| with P that row's **close** (the exit row too, not the exit fill price; on close fills they are equal). The engine and the MTM call the same `mtm.borrow_rows`, so the trade's borrow is the sum of its daily charges.
  - Non-US listing = a `*_USD` leg (the same predicate as the FX conversion): every non-US listing in the USD panel is a converted local leg; ADRs (SAP, BHP, …) and US listings of Canadian names (RY, TD, …) trade in USD on NYSE and pay the US rate (flat) or their tier (almgren). `--borrow-nonus` default 300 bp/yr = the highest borrow tier of `almgren_chriss_repricing.TIER_TABLE` (us_small_cap, aussie_local): the hard-to-borrow / locate cost of foreign lines is unknown here, so the high tier. The almgren model keeps its tier rates (canadian_local 120, european_local 150, aussie_local 300 bp/yr).
  - FX conversion applies to both models (2 bp per fill of a `*_USD` leg × its notional), never under `--legacy-flat-cost`.
  - `legacy_flat_cost` restores the pre-T8 cost per trade for either model (flat 2·(cost + slip), almgren 2·(c1 + c2) with leg 2's tier borrow); it combines with every other legacy flag; `legacy_spread_pnl` requires it (`ValueError`), the T3/T4 pattern.
